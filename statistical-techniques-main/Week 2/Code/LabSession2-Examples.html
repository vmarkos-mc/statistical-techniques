Area under the Normal Distribution Curve

  1. Approximately 95% of the area under the normal curve lies in the interval given by \(\mu \pm 2\sigma\).
  2. For \(\mu = 0\) and \(\sigma = 1\) (the standard normal distribution):
    • The upper interval limit is at the quantile 97.5.
    • The lower interval limit is at the quantile 2.5.
    • qnorm(0.975) gives 1.959964.
    • qnorm(0.025) gives -1.959964.
    • These values are approximately \(\pm 2\). Insert a code block to verify this.

99.7% of the Normal Curve’s Area

  2. Quantile limits are 0.15% and 99.85%.

Confidence Intervals

  1. 95% confidence interval for the population mean \(\mu\) is given by: \[ \bar{x} \pm 1.96 \times \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \]

  2. Example: The weight of eggs sold by a grocery store is normally distributed with a standard deviation of 18 grams. A sample of 20 eggs has an average weight of 42 grams.

# 95% confidence interval
mean_weight <- 42
sd_weight <- 18
n <- 20
error <- qnorm(0.975) * (sd_weight / sqrt(n))
ci_lower <- mean_weight - error
ci_upper <- mean_weight + error
ci_lower
## [1] 34.1113
ci_upper
## [1] 49.8887

Confidence Intervals when the Data is Normally Distributed

  1. 95% confidence interval for the sample mean when the variance is known: \[ \bar{x} \pm 1.96 \times \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \]

  2. Example with known standard deviation:

x2 <- c(238, 474, 359, 256, 481, 323, 151, 340, 284, 417, 424, 309, 139, 237, 269, 46, 350, 408, 223, 408, 286, 224, 238, 119, 343)
mean(x2) + qnorm(0.975) * (sd(x2)/sqrt(25))
## [1] 337.4194

Determination of Sample Size for Estimating a Population Mean

  1. Example: The weight of eggs sold by a grocery store is normally distributed with a standard deviation of 8 grams. Compute the sample size needed for a 99% confidence interval with an interval width of at most 2 grams.
sd_eggs <- 8
z_alpha <- qnorm(0.99)
epsilon <- 2
n <- (z_alpha * sd_eggs / epsilon)^2
n
## [1] 86.59031
